Executive Summary

Company formation in Belgium is the structured process through which a business presence is legally created, documented and made capable of operating within the Belgian commercial and regulatory system. It covers the choice of legal form, drafting and filing of a deed of incorporation, registration with public authorities, initial governance organisation and the core tax registrations needed before regular trading can begin. [web:8][web:11]

Operationally, company formation often starts with a decision about whether the business should be carried out through a limited liability company such as an SRL or SA, a cooperative company (SC), partnership forms or branch registration for a foreign enterprise. Founders assess liability, capital requirements, ownership flexibility, governance and administrative expectations before designing the legal structure that will hold contracts, assets and staff. [web:8][web:9]

The institutional environment is shaped by Belgian company law, the Business Courts, the Crossroads Bank for Enterprises, accredited business counters and the tax administration. The deed of incorporation is prepared (often as a notarial deed), filed with the competent Business Court, then registered so that the company can be entered in the Crossroads Bank for Enterprises and assigned an enterprise number, followed by VAT and employer registrations where required. [web:6][web:8][web:11]

Cross-border relevance is high because many Belgian entities involve foreign owners, group structures and activity across more than one country. Foreign companies may develop Belgian operations through branches or subsidiaries and must consider tax liability, regulatory conditions, registration in the Crossroads Bank for Enterprises and documentation requirements when entering Belgium. Practical company formation decisions therefore often integrate Belgian domestic rules with EU market context, international tax coordination and banking expectations. [web:5][web:6][web:11]

Purpose

The purpose of company formation in Belgium is to convert an intended business activity into a recognised legal and operational structure that can hold rights, enter contracts, interact with authorities and support commercial growth.

It exists to create clarity around ownership, liability, governance and registration status so that business activity can begin on a lawful, administratively workable and internationally credible basis.

A validly established Belgian business structure with appropriate deed of incorporation, registration, foundational documentation, governance arrangement and initial authority onboarding aligned to its planned commercial activity in Belgium and, where relevant, across borders. [web:8][web:11]

Process Flow

Process flow explains the typical sequence through which company formation occurs in Belgium. Practical details vary by legal form and founder profile, but the pattern usually moves from structure selection and financial planning to deed drafting, filing, registration in the CBE and tax onboarding. [web:6][web:8][web:11]

Step 1 — Structure and Intent Define the intended business model, ownership structure and operating footprint in Belgium, including whether the activity should be carried out through an SRL, SA, SC, partnership, sole proprietorship or Belgian branch of a foreign company. [web:8][web:9]
Step 2 — Legal Form Selection and Financial Plan Select the legal form, prepare a financial plan justifying capital or equity and estimating needs and revenues, and consider contributions in cash or kind. [web:8]
Step 3 — Deed of Incorporation Draft the deed of incorporation, including articles of association and required details, as an authentic (notarial) deed for common legal forms or a private deed for certain partnerships. [web:8]
Step 4 — Filing the Deed File the deed with the registrar of the competent Business Court within prescribed time limits, either physically or via electronic filing platforms; the deed is entered in legal entities records. [web:8]
Step 5 — Registration and Enterprise Number Registrar transmits identification details to the Crossroads Bank for Enterprises, which assigns an enterprise number; an extract is published in the Belgian Official Gazette. [web:6][web:8][web:18]
Step 6 — Registration Duties and Tax Onboarding Register the deed at FPS Finance and pay registration fees; arrange VAT, employer and tax registrations through FPS Finance and dedicated portals as required. [web:6][web:8][web:11]
Step 7 — UBO and Administrative Setup Register beneficial owners in the UBO register, set up access to e-government services such as e-Box Enterprise and CSAM, arrange banking, book-keeping and authority correspondence routines. [web:5][web:6][web:12]
Step 8 — Operational Launch Begin active operations once registration, tax status, banking, UBO and administrative arrangements are in place. [web:5][web:11]
